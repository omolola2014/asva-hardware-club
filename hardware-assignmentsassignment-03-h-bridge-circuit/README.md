## Operation of an H-Bridge Circuit
An H-bridge circuit is an electronic circuit used to control the direction of rotation of a DC motor by reversing the polarity of the voltage supplied to it. The circuit consists of four switches (S1, S2, S3, and S4), a DC motor, connecting wires, and a battery that serves as the power supply.
## Principle of Operation
The direction of rotation of the motor is controlled by opening and closing specific pairs of switches:
1. Anticlockwise Rotation: When switches S1 and S4 are open, while switches S2 and S3 are closed, current flows through the motor in one direction, causing it to rotate anticlockwise.
2. Clockwise Rotation: When switches S2 and S3 are open, while switches S1 and S4 are closed, the direction of current flow through the motor is reversed, causing it to rotate clockwise.
The H-bridge circuit enables the motor to rotate in both clockwise and anticlockwise directions by reversing the polarity of the voltage across its terminals. This makes it useful in applications requiring bidirectional motor control, such as robotics and electric vehicle drive systems.
